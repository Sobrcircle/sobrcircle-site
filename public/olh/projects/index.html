Renovations, repairs and emergency restoration

We manage the project. Licensed trades do the work.

One person coordinating the whole job, from the first inspection to the final reconciliation. We are not a general contractor, engineering firm or building inspector, and we don’t pretend to be.

  • Scope, quote, schedule, coordinate and procure
  • Inspect, document and verify the work
  • Manage tenant access, progress and deficiencies to completion

We don’t

  • Perform regulated construction ourselves
  • Act as the general contractor, engineer or inspector
  • Bury our fee inside a contractor’s invoice

What we take on

From one repair to a whole building brought back.

Where a job means several trades, a real budget or tenants in place, you get one manager coordinating it, not a phone full of contractor numbers.

  • Single repairs and turnovers

    One job or a vacant unit made rent-ready, managed the same disciplined way.

  • Unit renovations

    Kitchens, baths, flooring, paint and fixtures, to a leasable standard.

  • Common areas

    Entrances, hallways, lighting and lobbies.

  • Building systems

    Boiler, heating and plumbing, with the licensed trades qualified to do it.

  • Exterior and envelope

    Roof, windows and exterior work.

  • Capital programs

    Roofs, boilers, windows, mechanical, electrical and life-safety upgrades, scoped and sequenced.

  • Deferred-maintenance rehab

    Years of unfinished repairs triaged into a plan, worst risks first.

  • Staged repositioning

    Units renovated as they turn over, so the building improves without emptying it.

Planning work across a whole building? We stage capital work and renovate units as they turn over, so you spread the cost. Apartment buildings and multifamily.

How a project runs

Eight steps, every time.

Clearly defined, properly authorized, completed, documented and reported. Our job is to represent your interests until the approved work is done properly and within budget.

  1. 1

    Assessment and scope

    We inspect, record existing conditions with photos and measurements, flag urgent items and write a scope a contractor can quote accurately.

  2. 2

    Sourcing and vetting trades

    We find qualified contractors and check licensing, insurance, experience and references.

  3. 3

    Quotations, managed

    Trades quote a comparable scope. We check for missing work, exclusions and taxes, compare side by side and recommend.

  4. 4

    Budget and your approval

    An itemized budget: labour, materials, permits, disposal, contingency and HST. Nothing non-urgent goes past the approved limit without your sign-off.

  5. 5

    Scheduling and tenants

    We sequence the trades and arrange access. Where units are occupied, we handle lawful notice and service-interruption warnings.

  6. 6

    Supervision and reports

    Site visits and photo review of attendance, workmanship and materials, with updates and photos for you.

  7. 7

    Changes and cost control

    Hidden conditions are documented and quoted as change orders for your approval. Invoices are reviewed line by line.

  8. 8

    Closeout and warranty

    A deficiency list cleared before sign-off, a budget-versus-actual reconciliation, and warranty follow-up afterward.

What you receive: before and during

  • A clear description of the problem and the recommended action
  • A detailed scope of work
  • Contractor quotes and a side-by-side comparison
  • An itemized budget with HST and total
  • Our contractor recommendation and approval requests
  • Progress reports with photos, and schedule updates
  • Change-order requests and cost-to-date reporting

What you receive: at completion

  • Final inspection and deficiency follow-up
  • A final cost reconciliation: budget against actual, with variances explained
  • Invoice and payment records
  • Warranties, manuals, permits and inspection records
  • Recommended future maintenance or capital work

A complete, organized project history you keep.

Trades and permits

The right trade for each part. The approvals handled.

Paperwork is where owner-run renovations stall or get red-tagged. We work out what a job needs and manage it, so the work is legal and ready for inspection.

Trades we coordinate

  • General contractors and renovation crews
  • Electricians, for ESA-permitted work
  • Plumbers and drain work
  • HVAC technicians
  • Roofers and restoration companies
  • Painters, drywallers and flooring installers
  • Structural, floor levelling and subfloor
  • Landscapers, engineers and other specialists

If a job needs a trade that isn’t listed, we source it.

Permits and approvals

  • Building permits for structural and renovation work
  • Electrical (ESA) and plumbing permits
  • Engineering or professional inspections where required
  • Rooming-house and lodging-house licensing
  • Fire-safety requirements
  • City permits, including street-parking permits for the crew

We deal with the city and the trades, so you don’t have to learn the permit system.

How work is billed

Two ways work gets billed. No hidden third.

You always see the contractor’s real cost and our fee as separate lines. Nothing is marked up inside an invoice you can’t read.

Dispatched repairs

No management charge

For ordinary small repairs, we dispatch a contractor and you pay them directly. We coordinate it, with no added fee for routine work.

Managed projects

A fee agreed up front

When we run a real project, attending, inspecting, coordinating trades, procuring materials and handling the accounting, a project management fee applies on top of the contractor’s cost. Each project is quoted on its own scope and size.

  • Materials at cost

    Bought on your behalf and billed at what they cost.

  • You approve first

    An itemized budget before non-emergency work begins.

  • Reconciled at the end

    Budget against actual, with any variance explained.

Emergency restoration program

Damage at your rental? The first response is already covered.

A burst pipe, a roof leak, fire or storm damage. For properties we manage, the first response is part of the management you already pay for. If it grows into a full restoration, you choose how it’s run.

  1. 1

    We take the report and triage it

    You tell us once. We identify the urgent risks and start coordinating.

  2. 2

    First inspection, coordinated

    We arrange the first contractor or specialist to attend and report their findings.

  3. 3

    Authorized urgent protection

    Within your management authority: stopping active leaks or further damage and securing the unit.

  4. 4

    Your preliminary plan

    A Condition Assessment & Restoration Plan, written to be useful to you, an insurer or the municipality.

Included vs. what you pay. The first response, the first contractor or specialist inspection and your preliminary plan are included in regular management. The contractor’s work and materials are owner costs, as for any repair.

What the Condition Assessment & Restoration Plan sets out
  • What was reported, what we observed and what is confirmed, kept apart
  • The urgent risks, and what was done to protect people and the property
  • Known uncertainties and concealed-condition caveats, stated up front
  • A prioritized list: what’s urgent, what’s next, what can wait
  • Recommended next steps and the decision in front of you
  • Documentation to show an insurer or the municipality that action is underway

It is a preliminary assessment, not a guarantee of a hidden condition or of any outcome. Showing that action is underway is not a promise of compliance or of a particular result.

When a repair grows into a restoration, you choose.

Multi-trade, staged or invasive work, a vacant-unit rebuild or weeks of coordination: that’s when it stops being ordinary maintenance. Either option is fine with us.

Option A · Owner-led

You run it yourself

No project-management fee

We hand you the assessment and the initial contractor findings. Best if you have your own trades and the time.

  • You hire, direct, schedule, supervise and review the contractor
  • You approve and pay the contractor’s invoices
  • You handle poor workmanship, deficiencies, delays, disputes or claims directly
  • OLH does not supervise, inspect, manage or step into the project or a contractor dispute
  • OLH continues your regular management duties as normal

Option B · OLH-coordinated

We run it end to end

25% of approved contractor project costs

With a separately signed project authorization. Best for multi-trade and staged restoration.

  • Itemized quotes obtained, compared and recommended
  • Scope, phases, trades, materials and schedule coordinated
  • Budget and your approval limits tracked throughout
  • Tenant notice and access coordinated where it applies
  • Work documented and monitored against the approved scope
  • Agreed updates with photos and videos
  • Payment coordination and closeout records
  • Deficiencies pursued directly, with corrective trades if you authorize them

Fee: 25% of the approved contractor project costs, plus HST on OLH’s fee, for the authorized program work only, disclosed in writing before work begins.

Safety never waits on the decision

Whichever you choose, urgent safety and property-protection work continues under your existing management agreement.

If a contractor doesn’t deliver

Under Option B, we pursue deficiencies directly, including, where warranted, paralegal-supported correspondence, a demand or litigation letter. Formal proceedings are outside the 25% fee and need a separate engagement. Under Option A, a dispute is yours to handle.

How the Option B fee works
  • It applies only to authorized program work, never to routine maintenance
  • Scope, fee basis, limits and approval requirements are disclosed in writing before work begins
  • The sample authorization applies the fee to approved contractor invoices for labour and contractor-supplied materials
  • Separately purchased materials, permits, the contractor’s own HST and change orders are handled as your project authorization sets out
  • Option A carries no project-management fee at all

Each contractor remains responsible for their own technical work, licensing and workmanship under either option. Your signed project authorization governs the exact fee treatment for your restoration. No legal result is promised.

Questions about projects.

Are you the general contractor?

No. We coordinate, procure, schedule and oversee the contractors and licensed trades who do the work. We are not a general contractor, engineering firm or building inspector. Each contractor stays responsible for their own technical work, licensing and workmanship.

Who does the work, and do you just forward me their price?

Licensed, insured trades do the work. We don’t just pass along a price: we make sure trades quote a comparable scope, check each quote for missing work, exclusions and taxes, and recommend the most suitable contractor, which isn’t always the cheapest.

What if hidden damage turns up once work starts?

A quote reflects what was visible at inspection. If work uncovers structural, plumbing, electrical, moisture, rot or pest problems, we stop, document it, get a change order and your written approval before going on, except where emergency action is needed to protect people or the property.

Can you work in occupied buildings?

Yes. Common areas, building systems and exterior work go ahead with tenants in place, with lawful notice and access built into the schedule. Deeper unit renovations are usually staged to lawful vacancy, which also spreads the cost.

Does renovation help with a rent increase?

It can. Qualifying capital work, such as roofs, boilers, windows and major building systems, is documented with costs, dates and invoices so an above-guideline increase can be weighed properly. Renovation is never used to get around rent-control protections.

What happens when damage is first reported at a property you manage?

We take the report, identify the urgent risks, arrange the first contractor or specialist inspection, take authorized urgent steps to protect people and the property, and give you a preliminary Condition Assessment & Restoration Plan. That first response is part of regular management. The contractor’s work and materials are your costs, as with any repair.

Does OLH handle disputes with a contractor?

Under Option B, early resolution is included: raising deficiencies, arranging corrective trades where you authorize it and, where warranted, paralegal-supported correspondence or a demand letter. Formal proceedings, such as serving or filing a claim, are outside the coordination fee and need a separate engagement. No legal result is promised. Under Option A, a contractor dispute is yours to handle.
