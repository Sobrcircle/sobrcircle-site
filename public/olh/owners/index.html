Condo owners

Condo apartments and condo townhouses

Rental management for the person who owns the unit.

This is management of your unit and your tenancy. It is not management of the condominium corporation, the board or the common elements.

What’s different

Two people get paid before you do
Your lender and the condominium corporation are paid on schedule whether or not your tenant pays. One missed month comes out of your own account.
Two sets of rules apply
The tenancy runs under the Residential Tenancies Act. The declaration, by-laws and rules bind you as the owner and, through the lease, your tenant.
Many condo landlords are accidental
You moved, kept the unit, and now run a tenancy from your phone between meetings. That works until the day a legal deadline lands.

What we do: your unit, your tenant

  • Marketing, screening and the lease for your unit
  • Rent, deposits and a live owner ledger
  • Maintenance inside your unit, with photos and video
  • Notices and Landlord and Tenant Board work through our licensed paralegals
  • Coordination with your condominium corporation on your behalf

What we don’t do: the corporation’s side

  • Managing a condominium corporation or condo board
  • Reserve fund studies, budgets or common-expense collection
  • Common elements, amenities, lobby or grounds
  • Issuing status certificates (that’s the corporation’s manager)
  • Board meetings, AGMs or corporation governance

If you’re a board director looking for a firm to manage the building itself, we’re not that company, and we’ll tell you on the first call.

Compliance letters, rule complaints and chargebacks from the corporation come to us. If a rule breach needs to become a notice or a Board application, our licensed paralegals take it from there.

Questions owners ask first

Do you manage condominium corporations or condo boards?

No. We manage an individual condo unit for the owner who rents it out: the tenancy, the rent, the lease and the legal side. We don’t manage condominium corporations, boards, reserve funds or common elements. If you’re a board director looking for a firm to run the building itself, we’ll say so on the first call.

Who deals with the corporation’s rules, bookings and status certificates?

We do. We give your tenant the rules, file tenant-information forms where your corporation asks for them, book elevators for moves, track parking, lockers and fobs, and request status certificates from the corporation’s manager. Every corporation’s rules differ, so we read yours rather than assume.

Does the Residential Tenancies Act or the Condominium Act govern my tenancy?

Both matter. The tenancy runs under the Residential Tenancies Act like any other Ontario rental. The declaration, by-laws and rules made under the Condominium Act bind you as the owner and reach your tenant through the lease. General information, not legal advice about a specific building.

I moved and kept my condo. Can you take over mid-tenancy?

Yes, and it’s one of the most common files we open. We review the lease and the ledger, fix gaps in the paperwork, set your tenant up in the portal and take over rent and communication from the next cycle. A tenant dispute that already exists is taken on as a separate engagement.

Student rentals

University and college towns

Student rentals don’t fail slowly. They fail on one date.

Miss the leasing window and a room can sit empty for the whole 8- or 12-month cycle, because the students who would have rented it have signed somewhere else.

What’s different

The window is weeks, not months
Most student houses lease well ahead of the term. Once it starts, demand drops and a lower rent no longer brings it back.
Everything turns over at once
A five-bedroom house is five move-outs, five inspections, five sets of keys and five new leases, all in the same fortnight.
The person paying often isn’t the tenant
Parents and guarantors mean two sets of expectations, and a guarantee only helps if it was screened and written down at signing.
Per-room tenancies multiply the paperwork
Separate leases mean separate ledgers, separate arrears and separate notices. One house can produce the admin of a small building.

What we do about it

  • Listing timed to the student intake, on MLS® and REALTOR.ca through our brokerage
  • Guarantors screened exactly like applicants, with the guarantee written into the lease at signing
  • A ledger for every room, so you see which tenancy is short this month
  • The Ontario Standard Lease e-signed, so a student and a guarantor in other provinces can sign the same day
  • Dated move-in photos and documented repairs, so a damage claim rests on evidence
  • Noise and bylaw complaints handled with written warnings that build a proper file

We screen the guarantor too. That’s often who actually pays.

A first-year student usually has an empty credit file. That isn’t a red flag, which is why guarantors matter. Every applicant and every guarantor gets the same checks:

  • Equifax credit report and score
  • Government-issued ID verified
  • Employment and income verified
  • Bank statements and income stability reviewed
  • Previous landlord references
  • A search of past Landlord and Tenant Board orders
  • Application fraud check by cross-matching details

Students are tenants, with full rights

A fixed term doesn’t end the tenancy
When an 8- or 12-month term ends, the tenancy continues month to month unless the tenant gives proper written notice and leaves, or you both agree in writing to end it.
Off-campus isn’t exempt
Residences run by a college or university can be exempt from parts of the Residential Tenancies Act. An ordinary off-campus house or apartment is not.
Guarantors are lawful
Asking for a guarantor is common and sensible where an applicant has little income or credit history, as long as the guarantee is documented properly.

General information, not legal advice. Removing any tenant needs the correct notice, a Landlord and Tenant Board application and a hearing. The Board sets the pace.

Questions owners ask first

Does a student lease end automatically in April or August?

No. In Ontario a fixed-term lease doesn’t end the tenancy. When the term ends it continues month to month, unless the tenant gives proper written notice and leaves or you both agree in writing to end it. Plan turnover around notice, not the calendar. General information, not legal advice.

Can a parent guarantee a student’s lease?

Yes. Guarantors are common and lawful in student rentals. We screen the guarantor the same way we screen the applicant, then write the guarantee into the lease at signing, so it can actually be relied on later.

What if one roommate stops paying and the others don’t?

It depends on how the tenancy was set up. Separate per-room tenancies are separate files; a joint lease is one tenancy with shared liability. Either way the right notice has to be served correctly, and our licensed paralegals can handle it.

How is a house rented by the room priced?

We quote it for your house, after a short call about how many tenancies it has and how they’re set up.

First-time landlords

New and about-to-be Ontario landlords

Nobody hands you a rulebook. This is the rulebook.

You bought a rental, inherited a tenant, finished a basement suite, or kept the old house when you moved. Read this even if you never call us.

What’s different

Mistakes cost months, not fines
A wrong deposit, a missing lease form or an N4 with the wrong date is measured in months of your own rent.
The careful part comes first
Once you hand over keys, Ontario law protects the tenancy. The time to be thorough is before the lease is signed.
Waiting doesn’t help
The Board’s timeline starts when you file, not when the trouble started. Kindness is not a strategy, and the Board does not reward waiting.

What we do about it

  • Screening every adult applicant the same way, before the lease
  • The Ontario Standard Lease, prepared and e-signed
  • Rent tracked in a ledger from day one
  • N4 and N1 notices prepared properly
  • A licensed paralegal to talk to on every plan

Two units on one property

Renting a basement suite as well as the main floor, or both sides of a duplex? You don’t buy two plans. We count tenancies, so the first is at the first-tenancy rate and the second costs less: $29 a month on Landlord Shield, $79 on Property Management. See the calculator.

The 8 most expensive first-time mistakes.

Every one of these turns up in real files, most weeks. If you read nothing else, read these.

  1. 1Renting to the first nice person who shows up

    “They seemed great” isn’t screening. A real file is a credit report, verified income and employment, government ID, landlord references and a check of past LTB orders. After the lease, your options are slow and expensive.

  2. 2Using a lease you found online

    For most private residential tenancies entered into on or after April 30, 2018, Ontario’s Standard Form of Lease is mandatory. If a tenant asks for it in writing and doesn’t get it, they may be entitled to withhold up to one month’s rent. Clauses that contradict the Residential Tenancies Act are unenforceable, signed or not.

  3. 3Collecting a “damage deposit”

    Ontario allows a rent deposit, normally last month’s rent and capped at one rent period, with interest owed to the tenant each year, plus a refundable key deposit. A separate damage or security deposit isn’t allowed, and the rent deposit can’t be spent on repairs.

  4. 4Serving an N4 with the wrong dates or amounts

    The termination date has to be far enough out: at least 7 days for any tenancy since Bill 60 took effect on September 21, 2026, plus 5 days if mailed. The amount must be rent only, not NSF charges, late fees or utilities. Get either wrong and the application can be dismissed, and you start again.

  5. 5Not documenting the unit at move-in

    There’s no mandatory inspection form, so most first-timers skip it. A dated photo and video walkthrough of every room, acknowledged by the tenant, is the difference between a provable damage claim and your word against theirs.

  6. 6Letting arrears run “just one more month”

    You can serve an N4 the day after rent is late, and serving it doesn’t force anyone out. Most tenants simply pay. Landlords who wait three or four months to act are the ones who lose the most.

  7. 7Raising the rent the casual way

    A text saying “rent’s going up in January” isn’t a rent increase. You need Form N1, given at least 90 days ahead, and generally no more than once every 12 months. Most units are held to the yearly guideline. An improper increase can be void.

  8. 8Thinking the one-year lease ends the tenancy

    When the term ends, the tenancy continues month to month on the same terms. Ending it needs a lawful reason and the Board’s process. Changing locks, removing belongings or cutting utilities is illegal.

General information about Ontario residential tenancies, not legal advice, and the exceptions matter. Last reviewed 2026-11-11. See the Bill 60 guide, LTB forms and rent increases.

Your first tenancy, in order

Do these before you hand over keys. It’s the same sequence we follow for our own management clients.

  1. 1Confirm the unit is legal and safe for the use: zoning, fire separation, egress windows, working smoke and CO alarms
  2. 2Set the rent from real local comparables, not a guess
  3. 3Advertise properly and screen every adult applicant the same way
  4. 4Pull credit, verify income and ID, and call the previous landlord
  5. 5Use Ontario’s Standard Form of Lease, and read it before the tenant does
  6. 6Collect only a lawful rent deposit and, if you wish, a refundable key deposit
  7. 7Photograph and video every room on move-in day, dated and acknowledged
  8. 8Give the tenant a written way to request repairs, and keep every request
  9. 9Start a rent ledger on day one: memory is not evidence
  10. 10Confirm your insurance covers a rental, and require tenant insurance

Questions owners ask first

Can I collect a damage deposit in Ontario?

No. You may collect a rent deposit, normally last month’s rent and no more than one rent period, with interest owed to the tenant each year, and a refundable key deposit. A separate damage or security deposit isn’t allowed, and the rent deposit goes toward the last rent period, not repairs. General information, not legal advice.

My tenant’s one-year lease is ending. Do they have to move out?

No. When the term ends, the tenancy continues month to month on the same terms unless you both agree to a new term or the tenant gives proper notice. Ending a tenancy needs a lawful reason and the Board’s process. Changing locks or removing belongings is illegal.

I inherited a tenant when I bought the property. What now?

The tenancy came with the building, along with the tenant’s rent, rights and rent-increase history. Start by finding out exactly what you inherited: the lease, the rent, the last lawful increase, the deposit and any arrears. It’s a good use of a 15-minute call.

Is the 15-minute call really free?

Yes. Fifteen minutes with our team, no charge and no obligation. Bring your lease, your dates and your question.

Non-resident owners

Overseas and out-of-province owners

Own an Ontario rental from anywhere, without flying back for a hearing.

You can’t drive over when the furnace quits, or sit in a Landlord and Tenant Board hearing from another time zone. We’re your Ontario team, with an owner portal you can check any time.

What’s different

Distance
Arrears you hear about late, small leaks that become ceilings, and a vacancy you can’t show from abroad.
The hearing
The process that gets your property back happens in a hearing you can’t easily attend from another country.
The CRA
Rent paid to a non-resident owner is subject to withholding. Owners who never set it up find out at tax time.

NR4 and NR6, coordinated by your Ontario manager

General information, not tax advice

25% withholding on gross rent
A non-resident landlord’s agent in Canada is required to withhold 25% of gross rent and remit it to the CRA monthly. Gross, not net.
NR6: withholding on net instead
Filing an NR6 allows the withholding to be worked out on net rental income rather than gross.
NR4: the annual slip
An NR4 slip reporting the rent and the tax withheld is issued each year.

NR4 and NR6 filings are available on request on Property Management plans. We coordinate them and keep the records your accountant needs: every rent receipt, statement and expense is already in your owner portal. Owners in another Canadian province generally aren’t affected by the withholding rules.

We’re a property manager with licensed paralegals on the team, not tax advisors. The specifics of your situation belong with your accountant, and we’ll work alongside them.

Questions owners ask first

Can I own and rent out an Ontario property while living outside Canada?

Yes. What changes is the paperwork and the logistics: rent paid to a non-resident owner is subject to non-resident withholding, and you can’t run showings, repairs or a hearing from another country. We act as your Ontario manager, and you watch rent, statements and repairs in your owner portal.

Who attends the Landlord and Tenant Board hearing if I live abroad?

On Property Management + Legal Guarantee (from $199 a month), our licensed paralegals handle eligible new LTB matters, including hearing representation, so you don’t fly back. Filing fees and outside costs are extra. On the other plans, a qualifying new non-payment case is available at the member rate, from $995.

How do I know the repair I paid for was done?

Every maintenance job sits in your owner portal with photos or video of the work, next to the invoice. You look at the picture instead of taking anyone’s word for it.

I live in another province, not another country. Does any of this apply?

The withholding rules are for non-residents of Canada, so an owner in another province generally isn’t affected. Confirm with your accountant. Everything else applies the same way: you still can’t attend a hearing from Calgary, and you still need someone looking at the property.
