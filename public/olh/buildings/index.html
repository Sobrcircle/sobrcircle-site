Is your building distressed?

A structured takeover. Not a hopeful handover.

Distress rarely shows up as one problem. If several of these are true, the building needs stabilization before ordinary management.

  • Arrears you can’t fully account for

    Chronic late or partial payment, and a rent roll that no longer matches the ledgers.

  • Expenses leaking out

    Boiler inefficiency, water intrusion, utility waste and repeat repairs.

  • Inconsistent or unenforceable leases

    Files passed between managers, with missing documents.

  • Problem tenancies left alone

    Smoking, disturbances and non-compliance that were never enforced.

  • Vacant units sitting idle

    Un-renovated, un-marketed units losing revenue every month.

  • Legal exposure in the files

    Liability a tenant may find before you do.

The stabilization plan

  1. 1Day one

    Takeover

    A formal transition. Communications, rent, keys and records move under one accountable team, and tenants know the building is professionally managed.

  2. 2Weeks 1–6

    Diagnosis

    Every tenancy file audited, the rent roll reconciled against real ledgers, and the building inspected end to end.

  3. 3Months 2–4

    Triage and control

    Expense leaks first: heating, water, utilities. Vendors and on-site staffing reviewed. Arrears enforced and tenancies sorted by risk.

  4. 4Months 3–6

    Recovery

    Vacant units scoped, renovated and leased. Below-market tenancies repositioned lawfully. An AGI strategy where capital work qualifies.

  5. 5Handover

    Stabilized

    Systems, documentation and enforcement in place. The building moves to day-to-day management with owner-portal visibility.

Legal throughout. Notices, non-payment, LTB applications and enforcement are handled by the licensed paralegals on our team. Capital work and unit renovations run through our project management service.

Lease audits and enforcement

Defensible files. Lawful enforcement.

Buildings that were self-managed or passed between managers almost always carry lease errors. We audit every unit, fix the paperwork, then enforce consistently. The aim isn’t conflict; it’s restoring professional standards.

What a lease audit finds

  • Lease terms that differ between units
  • Outdated or non-compliant clauses
  • Incorrect rent figures or missing documents
  • Improper or undocumented rent increases
  • Missing addenda or unenforceable provisions
  • Paperwork gaps that weaken an enforcement file

What enforcement addresses

  • Chronic non-payment or persistent late payment
  • Smoking and prohibited activity in units
  • Unauthorized occupants, alterations or equipment
  • Repeated lease violations
  • Legacy tenancies paying well below market
  • Long-standing issues ignored by prior management

Below-market rent alone is not a ground.

Every termination needs a lawful basis under the Residential Tenancies Act and goes through the Board’s process, which sets its own pace. For long-term repositioning, renovation and rent changes follow lawful turnover, paced to your objectives and risk tolerance.

Above-guideline increases (AGIs): when they’re worth it

An AGI can be available where qualifying capital work maintains, replaces or extends the life of the building. Our paralegals can assess it and run the L5 application: documentation, evidence, tenant communication and filing.

Are AGIs always worth pursuing? Not always. Recoverable increases are often limited, so we recommend one only when the return justifies the work. Renegotiating leases unit by unit alongside renovations is often faster.

Examples of qualifying capital work

  • Roof replacement or major roof rehabilitation
  • Boiler replacement or significant modification
  • Windows and building envelope
  • Major mechanical, electrical or life-safety upgrades

General information, not legal advice. Whether a cost qualifies, and how much can be recovered, is decided by the Landlord and Tenant Board.

Questions about buildings.

Do you manage large apartment buildings?

Yes, from duplexes and small portfolios up to apartment buildings with hundreds of units. Our systems, staffing and legal framework are set up for buildings with dozens of tenancies, not only single rentals.

Do you work with investors who are still buying a building?

Yes. During an acquisition we review the tenant files, leases, rent ledgers and operational risks, so you understand what you’re buying before takeover. The review is part of the management engagement, not sold on its own. As a licensed brokerage, List It Realty Brokerage Inc. can also support the purchase.

Why does the rent roll need checking against the ledgers?

Rent rolls show expected income; rent ledgers show what was actually collected. Reconciling the two over 12 to 24 months often turns up arrears, side agreements, chronic late payers or units that never appear in a listing package.

Do you handle evictions in-house?

Our team includes licensed Ontario paralegals. Notices, applications, hearings and enforcement are handled by them, under a separate written engagement with Landlord Pro Legal Services. The Board sets the pace, so no timeline is promised.

Will you remove problem tenants?

Where there are lawful grounds, our paralegals pursue the right process. It is never indiscriminate: every termination needs a legitimate basis under the Residential Tenancies Act. Below-market rent alone is not a ground.

Are above-guideline increases always worth pursuing?

Not always. An AGI takes significant documentation and time, and recoverable increases are often limited. We recommend one only when the expected return justifies the effort. Renegotiating leases unit by unit alongside renovations is often faster.

Do you mark up maintenance or repairs?

No hidden markups. Contractor invoices go to you directly, and a project management fee applies only when we actively coordinate and oversee the work, agreed up front. You see where every dollar goes.

How fast will we see a change in a distressed building?

The stabilization plan is scoped around the first months, because diagnosis and control come before results. Owners can expect change in operating control, expense leakage, collections, vacancy and documentation in that window. It’s an expectation from the approach, not a guaranteed figure.

How is a building priced?

Each building is quoted on its own: its condition, its tenant profile and how much legal work it needs. Up to ten tenancies, the price is on our pricing page.
