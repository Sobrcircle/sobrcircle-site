Guide · Rent increases

Raising the rent in Ontario.

How much, how often, and how much notice. For most units: up to the guideline, once every 12 months, after 90 days’ written notice on an N1.

Book a 15-minute callSee the guideline by year

  • 1.9% for 2027

    The guideline for increases that take effect during 2027, set by the province.

  • 90 days’ notice

    Serve a written N1 at least 90 days before the new rent starts.

  • Once every 12 months

    And not in a tenant’s first 12 months. Miss the window and you wait a full year.

The guideline, year by year

Serving an N1 now? Use 1.9%.

An N1 needs at least 90 days’ notice, so one served now takes effect in 2027 at the earliest. The 2027 guideline applies to increases that take effect during 2027.

How to work it out

Multiply the current rent by the guideline. That’s the most you can add.

2027 at 1.9%
$2,000 + $38 = $2,038
2026 at 2.1%
$2,000 + $42 = $2,042

The exact new rent goes on the N1, with an effective date at least 90 days after you serve it.

The guideline is set each year by Ontario’s Ministry of Municipal Affairs and Housing and announced around late summer of the year before. Confirm the current figure on ontario.ca.

The rules

Three rules, and the mistakes that break them.

The wrong form or short notice makes the increase invalid.

  1. 1

    Within the guideline

    For most rent-controlled units, no more than the year’s guideline unless the LTB approves more.

  2. 2

    Once every 12 months

    No more than one increase in 12 months, and none until the tenant has lived there 12 months.

  3. 3

    90 days on the N1

    At least 90 days’ written notice on the official N1, served by an approved method.

Common mistakes

  • Giving less than 90 days’ notice, or notice that isn’t on the official N1.
  • Raising the rent twice in 12 months, or in the tenant’s first 12 months.
  • Going above the guideline without the Board’s approval.
  • Using an N1 for a unit that’s exempt from the guideline (that’s an N2), or the reverse.
  • Getting the new rent wrong. Work it out from the current rent and the guideline for the year it takes effect.
  • Serving by an invalid method, such as taping it to the door or sending a text.

Serving the N1

The N1 is served like any other notice: in person, in the mailbox, under the door, by courier, by mail, or by email with the tenant’s written consent. By regular mail it counts as served 5 days after mailing, so allow for that in the 90 days.

How to serve a notice

A mistimed or badly served N1 costs you a full year of the increase.

Exemptions and above-guideline increases

When the guideline doesn’t apply, and when it isn’t enough.

N2

Exempt units

Units first occupied after November 15, 2018 are generally exempt from the guideline cap. They’re served an N2, Notice of Rent Increase (Unit Partially Exempt), not an N1.

  • The 90 days’ written notice still applies.
  • So does once every 12 months.
  • Some care homes and social housing follow separate rules.

L5

Above the guideline

Going above the guideline needs the LTB’s approval, through an Application for an Above Guideline Rent Increase (L5). These applications are contested and closely scrutinised.

  • For major capital work (roof, boiler or mechanical, windows, elevators, life-safety systems) or extraordinary municipal tax or utility cost increases.
  • At most 3% above the guideline a year, spread over up to 3 years if the justified increase is larger.
  • Invoices, contracts, permits and proof of payment for every item.
  • Since July 1, 2026: serve the documents within 7 days of the Board’s direction, and file a certificate of service within 5 days of serving.

A separate form, the N10, records an above-guideline increase the tenant agrees to. Check the L5 filing fee on the LTB site.

Deposits and interest

Last month’s rent, and the interest on it.

A rent increase touches the deposit too. Interest on it is owed each year, at the guideline rate.

What you can collect
A rent deposit equal to one rental period’s rent, usually one month. It can only be applied to the last month’s rent, not to damage or cleaning.
What you can’t
Damage deposits, key deposits beyond the actual replacement cost, and automatic cleaning fees.
Interest
Paid to the tenant on the rent deposit each year, at the guideline rate.
After an increase
The deposit can be topped up to the new rent when the increase takes effect. LandlordEzy does it in one step.

Questions about rent increases.

What is the 2027 rent increase guideline?

1.9%, for increases that take effect during 2027. Because an N1 needs at least 90 days’ notice, an N1 served now takes effect in 2027 at the earliest, so 1.9% is the figure to use.

What was the 2026 guideline?

2.1%, for increases that took effect during 2026. The guideline was 2.5% in both 2025 and 2024.

How much can I raise the rent?

For most units, no more than the guideline, once every 12 months, after a written N1 served at least 90 days ahead. On $2,000 a month, 1.9% is $38, for a new rent of $2,038.

How much notice do I need to give?

At least 90 days’ written notice on the official N1 form. Rent can only go up once every 12 months, and not in a tenant’s first 12 months.

Which units are exempt from the guideline?

Units first occupied after November 15, 2018 are generally exempt from the cap. They’re served an N2 rather than an N1, and the 90-day and 12-month rules still apply. Some care homes and social housing follow separate rules.

Can I raise the rent above the guideline?

Only with LTB approval, through an above-guideline increase application (L5), for major capital work or extraordinary municipal tax or utility cost increases. These are contested. A paralegal can tell you whether your costs qualify.

Do I owe interest on last month’s rent?

Yes. You pay the tenant interest on the rent deposit each year, at the guideline rate. The deposit can be topped up to the new rent when an increase takes effect.

Can LandlordEzy prepare the N1 for me?

Yes, on Landlord Shield. It pre-fills the guideline, the new rent and the earliest legal effective date, and fills the official N1 for you to e-sign and serve. Once served, the increase applies to your rent ledger on the effective date. The software doesn’t guarantee a notice is valid; check the current guideline and any exemption before you serve.
