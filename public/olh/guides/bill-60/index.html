Guide · Bill 60

Bill 60: what changed for Ontario landlords.

The Fighting Delays, Building Faster Act, 2025 is in force. What it changed for the N4, N12 and N13, for arrears hearings and deadlines, and the few pieces still waiting.

Book a 15-minute callSee what changed

  1. November 27, 2025

    Royal Assent

    Bill 60 became law. Its Residential Tenancies Act changes arrived in two waves.

  2. July 1, 2026

    The first wave

    The 15-day review window, the Payment Agreement form, doubled fines, AGI service deadlines and tenant air conditioners.

  3. September 21, 2026

    The headline changes

    The 7-day N4, new forms, the 50% arrears rule, N12 compensation, persistent late payment and tighter relief from eviction.

The trigger-date rule. Each September 21 rule is keyed to a trigger date: the day a notice is served, an application is filed, or an order is issued. A notice served on or before September 20, 2026 stays on the old rules and doesn’t need to be served again.

Not yet in force, or dropped

  • Not proclaimed

    Renoviction report

    A qualified person’s report with a repair or renovation N13 (RTA s. 50(3)) is not yet required.

  • Not set

    Empty-unit presumption period

    The period after which an empty unit is presumed a bad-faith N12 (s. 57(6.1)) hasn’t been set. The 60-day move-in rule is what applies today.

  • Dropped

    Fixed-term roll-over

    Ending the automatic month-to-month continuation of fixed-term leases was removed before Royal Assent. Fixed-term leases still roll over.

What it means for your notices

The N4, N8, N12 and N13 now.

These apply to notices you serve today.

N4

Non-payment of rent

  • Termination date at least 7 days after service, for any tenancy type. Don’t count the day you serve.
  • Add 5 days by mail, 1 by courier.
  • The tenant can still void it by paying everything owing.
  • N4s served on or before September 20 keep their 14-day date.
  • Use the 2026/09 form, and state the rent owing exactly: Guideline 11 now says so expressly.
The N4 in the forms guide

N8

Persistent late payment

  • More than 7 days late at least 3 times in any 6-month period.
  • Payments you applied to older arrears don’t count as late.
  • The Board can still find persistence on other facts.
  • A ledger of due dates and paid dates is the evidence.
The N8 in the forms guide

N12

You, family or a buyer moving in

  • 60 days’ notice with one month’s compensation, or 120 days or more with none, for your own or a family member’s use.
  • A purchaser’s N12 always owes compensation.
  • Whoever moves in must occupy within 60 days of the termination date, or bad faith is presumed on a T5.
  • Delaying an eviction on an N12 now turns on whether delay would be unfair.
The N12 in the forms guide

N13

Demolition, conversion or major repairs

  • After a renovation N13: a written estimated completion date, updates, a completion notice and 60 days to reoccupy.
  • The T5 deadline is the later of 2 years after move-out or 6 months after the work finishes.
  • The renoviction report requirement is not yet in force.
The N13 in the forms guide

Hearings and deadlines

The numbers to diarize.

Shorter timelines reward landlords whose paperwork is exact.

  • 7 days

    The N4 minimum

    For any tenancy type, from the day after service.

  • 7 days before

    The 50% payment

    On L1s filed since September 21, a tenant raising issues pays at least half the arrears to you this far ahead of the hearing. Issue a receipt.

  • 15 days

    Asking for a review

    A request to review an LTB order is due within 15 days of the order. Diarize it the day the order arrives.

  • 60 days

    Moving in, or moving back

    An N12 occupant moves in within 60 days of the termination date. After a renovation N13, the tenant gets 60 days to reoccupy.

  • 7 + 5 days

    AGI service

    Serve above-guideline documents within 7 days of the direction, and file the certificate of service within 5 days.

  • 2 years

    T5 after an N13

    Or 6 months after the work finishes, whichever is later.

New forms

Eleven forms, version 2026/09.

The LTB reissued the N4, N5, N6, N8, N12, N13, L1, L2, L9, L10 and T5. Old versions stop being accepted on November 30, 2026. Replace any saved copies with the current versions from the LTB’s own site.

What to do now

Six things to change on your files.

  • Use the 2026/09 forms, and count 7 days on an N4 without the day of service. How to serve
  • Issue a receipt for any 50% payment a tenant makes before an L1 hearing.
  • For an own-use N12, choose between 60 days with compensation and 120 days without.
  • Keep a ledger of due dates and paid dates, the evidence an N8 needs.
  • Diarize the 15-day review window the day an order arrives.
  • Put any repayment plan on the LTB’s Payment Agreement form.

Bill 60 questions.

Is Bill 60 in force?

Yes, in two waves: July 1, 2026 and September 21, 2026. A few provisions, the renoviction report and the empty-unit presumption period, are not yet proclaimed.

How many days’ notice does an N4 need now?

At least 7 days for any tenancy type, for N4s served on or after September 21, 2026. Don’t count the day of service. Add 5 days by mail or 1 by courier. Notices served on or before September 20 keep their 14-day date. Use the 2026/09 N4.

What is the 50% arrears rule?

For L1 applications filed on or after September 21, 2026, a tenant who wants to raise their own issues at the arrears hearing must first pay the landlord at least half the arrears claimed, at least 7 days before the hearing, and give written notice of the issues. The money goes to the landlord directly, not to the LTB. Issue a receipt.

Does Bill 60 change N12 compensation?

Yes. An own-use N12 with 120 or more days’ notice, ending on a period end, needs no compensation or alternative unit. With less than 120 days, or for a purchaser’s N12, one month’s compensation still applies. Whoever moves in must take occupancy within 60 days of the termination date.

What counts as persistent late payment?

Rent received more than 7 days after the due date at least 3 times in any 6-month period. Payments applied to older arrears don’t count as late. The Board may still find persistence on other facts. This is the ground for an N8.

Which LTB forms changed?

The N4, N5, N6, N8, N12, N13, L1, L2, L9, L10 and T5 were reissued as version 2026/09. Previous versions are no longer accepted from November 30, 2026.

Can a tenant still delay or set aside an eviction order?

Only with the landlord’s consent, or where the Board finds there are compelling reasons and that delay would not be unfair (for an N12 or N13, the not-unfair test only). Set-aside motions on ex parte orders can no longer rely on changes in the tenant’s circumstances after the agreement.

What is still not in force?

The N13 renoviction report requirement and the prescribed empty-unit presumption period. The change to fixed-term roll-over was dropped from the bill entirely, so fixed-term leases still continue month to month.
