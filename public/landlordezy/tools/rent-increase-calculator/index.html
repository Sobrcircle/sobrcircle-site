How it’s calculated

An Ontario rent increase has to clear three rules:

  • 12 months since the last increase, or since the tenancy began.
  • 90 days’ written notice on the LTB’s Form N1. If you mail it, it counts as given five days after mailing.
  • The guideline for the year the increase takes effect: 2.1% in 2026 and 1.9% in 2027, unless the unit is exempt.

Units first occupied as residential units after November 15, 2018 are exempt from the guideline, but not from the notice and timing rules.

Sources: Ontario.ca: residential rent increases · Residential Tenancies Act, ss. 116 and 119

Common questions

What is the rent increase guideline for 2027?

1.9%, for increases that take effect between January 1 and December 31, 2027. It was 2.1% in 2026 and 2.5% in 2023, 2024 and 2025.

Which units are exempt from the guideline?

Units occupied for the first time for residential purposes after November 15, 2018 are not covered by the guideline. You still need 90 days’ written notice and 12 months between increases.

Can I increase the rent above the guideline?

Only with an LTB order (an above-guideline increase, Form L5) for things like major capital work or large increases in municipal taxes, or by agreement in limited cases.

What if my notice is late?

The increase can’t take effect until 90 days after the notice is given. The calculator shows the later date; the 12-month clock still runs from the last increase.
