How it’s calculated

Each year, a landlord owes interest on a rent deposit at the rent increase guideline in effect when the payment is due: 2.1% in 2026 and 1.9% in 2027.

When the rent has gone up, the landlord can keep that interest to top the deposit up toward the new rent, and can require the tenant to pay the rest of the difference. If interest is owed and not paid, the tenant can deduct it from rent.

Sources: Residential Tenancies Act, s. 106

Common questions

Do I have to pay interest on a last month’s rent deposit?

Yes. Every year, the landlord owes interest on the deposit at the rent increase guideline in effect when the payment is due.

Can I keep the interest to top up the deposit?

If the rent has gone up, yes: the interest can be applied to bring the deposit up toward the new rent, and you can ask the tenant to pay any remaining difference.

What happens if I don’t pay the interest?

The tenant may deduct the unpaid interest from a later rent payment.
