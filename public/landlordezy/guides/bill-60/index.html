Bill 60: what changed for Ontario landlords

The biggest update to Ontario’s rental rules in years took effect on September 21, 2026. Here’s what applies to notices and applications today, in plain English.

Reviewed October 15, 2026·6 min read·LandlordEzy team

The short version

The changes come from the Fighting Delays, Building Faster Act (Bill 60) and the Helping Homebuyers, Protecting Tenants Act, 2023 (Bill 97). Since September 21, 2026:

  • An N4 for unpaid rent needs 7 days’ notice, down from 14.
  • To raise other issues at an arrears hearing, a tenant must first pay you half the arrears you claimed.
  • An N12 for your own or a family member’s use with 120 days’ notice needs no compensation.
  • Whoever moves in after an N12 must do so within 60 days.
  • “Persistent late payment” now has a definition.
  • Relief from eviction is harder to get.
  • Ten LTB forms were updated; old versions stop being accepted on November 30, 2026.

N4: 7 days instead of 14

For an N4 given on or after September 21, 2026, the termination date must be at least 7 days after you give the notice. Don’t count the day you give it. Add one business day if you send it by courier, and five days if you mail it.

Nothing changed about voiding: if the tenant pays everything owing by the termination date, the notice is void. Try the N4 date calculator to get the date right.

Arrears hearings: half the arrears first

For non-payment applications filed on or after September 21, 2026, a tenant who wants to raise other issues at the hearing, such as maintenance or tenant rights, must pay you half of the arrears claimed in your application at least seven days before the hearing.

The money goes directly to you. It can’t be paid to the LTB in trust. Give a receipt and record the payment in your ledger so the amount owing stays accurate.

N12: no compensation at 120 days’ notice

When you need a unit for yourself or a family member (Reason 1 on the N12), you don’t have to pay one month’s compensation or offer another unit if the termination date is at least 120 days after you give the notice. The date must still fall on the last day of a rental period, or the last day of a fixed term.

Give less than 120 days and compensation is still required. The minimum notice for an N12 is 60 days.

Whoever the notice is for must take occupancy within 60 days of the termination date in the N12. If not, bad faith is presumed if the tenant files a T5 application.

N13: clearer duties during renovations

After an N13 for repairs or renovations, a landlord must now:

  • tell the tenant the estimated completion date without delay,
  • update the tenant if the timeline changes,
  • confirm in writing when the work is finished, and
  • give the tenant 60 days’ notice to move back in.

A tenant’s T5 bad-faith application can now be filed up to two years after they move out, or six months after the renovations are finished, whichever is later.

N8: “persistently late” is now defined

Rent is persistently late when it’s received more than seven days after the due date, at least three times within a six-month period. A payment doesn’t count as late just because you applied it to older arrears, and the Board can still find persistent lateness in other circumstances.

You can only prove this with accurate records. Keep a ledger that shows the date each payment actually arrived.

Relief from eviction is harder to get

The Board can now delay or refuse an eviction only if doing so would not be unfair to the landlord or other tenants and there are compelling reasons, or if the landlord consents.

New forms: switch before November 30

The N5, N6, N8, N12, N13, L1, L2, L9, L10 and T5 were updated on September 21, 2026. The LTB stops accepting the previous versions on November 30, 2026. Download forms fresh from Tribunals Ontario each time rather than reusing saved copies.

Your checklist

  1. Delete saved copies of the ten updated forms and download current versions.
  2. Use 7 days for every N4 given on or after September 21, 2026, plus mail or courier days.
  3. Keep a ledger with the actual date each payment arrived.
  4. Plan own-use N12s with at least 120 days’ notice, ending on the last day of a rental period.
  5. Make sure whoever moves in after an N12 does so within 60 days.
  6. Receipt any half-arrears payment a tenant makes before an L1 hearing.
