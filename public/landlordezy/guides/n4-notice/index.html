How to serve an N4 in Ontario

The N4 is the first step when a tenant doesn’t pay rent. Get the amounts and the date right, serve it properly, and you can apply to the Landlord and Tenant Board the day after it expires.

Reviewed October 15, 2026·7 min read·LandlordEzy team

The short answer

Give an N4 once rent is due and unpaid. The termination date must be at least 7 days after you give it, plus one business day by courier or five days by mail. If the tenant pays everything owing by then, the notice is void; if not, you can file an L1 the next day.

When you can give an N4

You can give an N4 once rent is due and hasn’t been paid in full. If rent is due on the 1st and isn’t paid, the earliest day to give the notice is the 2nd. The tenant must still be living in the unit.

If you want to collect the money without ending the tenancy, the LTB’s Form L9 is the alternative. If the tenant has already moved out, use Form L10.

Step 1: Work out the rent owing

The table on page three of the N4 lists, for each rental period with unpaid rent:

  • the period (from and to dates),
  • the rent charged,
  • the rent paid, and
  • the rent owing.

Only rent belongs on the N4. Rent includes the basic rent plus anything the tenant pays you separately for services, such as parking or a seasonal air-conditioning fee. A utility bill the tenant pays to the utility isn’t rent, but a flat monthly utility charge paid to you is. Don’t include the last month’s rent deposit or an NSF fee.

If more than three periods are owing, you can combine earlier periods in the first rows, but the last row must show the most recent period. The total on page three must match the amount on page one.

Step 2: Set the termination date

The termination date must be at least 7 days after you give the notice, not counting the day you give it. If you hand it over on March 3, the earliest termination date is March 10. Mailed on March 3, it’s March 15, because mail adds five days. A courier adds one business day.

Step 3: Names, address and signature

Name every tenant, plus any subtenant or assignee. You don’t need to name other occupants, such as children or guests. Include the full address with the unit number and postal code, then sign as the landlord or the landlord’s representative.

Step 4: Serve it

You can give the N4 by:

  • handing it to the tenant, or to an adult in the unit,
  • leaving it in the mailbox or where mail is normally delivered,
  • sliding it under the door or through a mail slot,
  • fax, courier or mail.

You can’t give an N4 by taping it to the door. Keep a copy of exactly what you gave the tenant.

Step 5: What happens next

If the tenant pays everything owing by the termination date, the notice is void. If they don’t pay or move out, you can file an L1 starting the day after the termination date. You’ll file a copy of the N4 and a Certificate of Service showing when and how you gave it.

For L1 applications filed on or after September 21, 2026, a tenant who wants to raise other issues at the hearing must first pay you half of the arrears claimed, at least seven days before the hearing.

Mistakes that get N4s thrown out

  • The total on page one doesn’t match the table on page three.
  • Non-rent amounts, like a deposit or NSF fee, are included.
  • The termination date is too early for how the notice was delivered.
  • A tenant’s name is missing.
  • The notice was posted on the door.
  • An old version of a form was used. Always download the current one.
