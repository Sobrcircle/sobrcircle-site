SobrCircle: the sobriety app built by someone in recovery

The sobriety app built by someone in recovery. Recovery is harder alone. You don't have to be. Count your days, find a meeting tonight, and stay close to the people who show up. SobrCircle (say it “sober circle”) was made in Windsor, Ontario.

Find recovery meetings near you

195,000+ meetings on one map, including AA, NA and CA meetings, with the time, the place and live navigation to the door. Drive, walk, bike or take transit, and see community events on the same map. New city, same recovery. Find meetings near you.

Your circle, people you choose

Add the people you trust with a private 6-character code. There's no limit on your circle, and you decide who's in it. Start with the people who answer at 2 AM.

Rooms and events

Recovery happens between meetings too. Join a public room to talk with people who get it, or join a private room of up to 50. Group chats keep up to 12 of your closest in one thread. Find members near you who've chosen to be discoverable, make yourself discoverable when you're ready, and find community events close to home. The sober community.

A sobriety tracker that celebrates with you

Count your days sober, see how close your next milestone is, and earn a badge at 24 hours, every month of your first year, 18 months and every year after. Your circle hears about it when you get there. How the sobriety tracker works · Sobriety calculator.

Daily check ins

Some days you need to say it out loud. A check in is a moment of honesty for you and for someone who needs to hear they're not the only one.

A private journal

A safe place to process the hard days and track your progress. Never visible to anyone.

Free to download, with no ads

No ads and no data selling. Tracking your days, finding meetings, one to one messages and joining any room or event cost nothing, and you can sign up without a credit card. Supporters can create their own rooms and events, which helps cover server costs.

Why SobrCircle exists

Founder Ben Moradi struggled with addiction for most of his life. When he started getting better, there was no space built for the daily part of recovery, so he built one. His poetry memoir, Twenty Four, tells the story behind the app.
